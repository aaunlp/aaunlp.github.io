Research culture

Ambitious, collaborative, and supportive.

We want people to develop their own ideas and research programmes—and to do better work together than they could alone.

Our culture is designed to make expertise visible, feedback routine, expectations clear, contributions recognised, and collaboration easier. The purpose is to support excellent research, not to add process for its own sake.

Seven values

What we try to make practical

These principles are adapted from the group's working handbook. The public summary focuses on the experience we want to create, rather than internal procedures.

01

Rigour

We pursue important questions, use methods and evidence suited to them, interpret results honestly, and change course when the evidence calls for it.

02

Feedback

We share work while it can still change and treat specific, useful feedback as a collective responsibility—not a scarce favour.

03

Clarity

We make roles, expectations, commitments, authorship, and important decisions explicit, then revisit them when circumstances change.

04

Responsibility

We take initiative and ownership, develop scientific judgement, ask for advice when needed, and raise blockers early. Independence is not isolation.

05

Pace

We maintain momentum through learning, decisions, and completed research stages. Quality and sustainability come before any single venue deadline.

06

Recognition

We recognise papers and grants—and the mentoring, feedback, datasets, code, coordination, and care that make the work possible.

07

Trust

We should be able to share unfinished ideas, failed experiments, and disagreement openly, with respect and a commitment to an inclusive environment.

In practice

Freedom to develop. Structure when it helps.

Independent research identities

Assistant professors, postdocs, and PhD fellows need space to develop questions and collaborations that are genuinely their own.

Feedback before work hardens

Supervision, paper discussions, reading groups, and informal exchange should help improve a question or design while meaningful changes remain possible.

Protected research time

Recurring activities should support research rather than crowd it out. Most working time for research-focused roles should remain available for core work.

Constructive disagreement

Psychological safety does not mean avoiding criticism or lowering standards. It makes honest scientific disagreement possible.
