Research culture

Ambitious, collaborative, and supportive.

We want people to develop their own ideas and research programmes—and to do better work together than they could alone.

Our culture is designed to make expertise visible, feedback routine, expectations clear, contributions recognised, and collaboration easier. The purpose is to support excellent research, not to add process for its own sake.

In practice

Five principles for everyday research.

The concise formulations come from AAU-NLP Start Here: a practical bridge between the values and day-to-day decisions.

Quality before deadlines

A deadline does not make a paper ready. We submit when the claim, evidence, record, and author review are strong enough—not because a date has arrived. Quality-first is not perfection-first: once the paper is ready, it should move.

Clarity before assumption

Roles, decisions, commitments, feedback timing, and authorship should be explicit and revisited when the work changes.

Ask early; help where you can

Blockers and changing circumstances should be raised early. Feedback is a shared contribution, while requests may be accepted, declined, or renegotiated according to expertise and workload.

Credit follows contribution

Contributions and author order should be discussed early and revisited as work develops. Every author must approve submission, and work beyond publication lists should be recognised.

Sustainable pace matters

Research time should be protected and work should fit agreed hours. Meeting attendance, physical visibility, or instant responsiveness is not a proxy for contribution.

The collective system

Ambition without a tournament culture.

Excellent research depends on individual scientific judgement and on a group that makes expertise, support, credit, and principled disagreement available.

01

Colleagues, not competitors

Another member's success does not diminish our own; it can strengthen the group and create opportunities for others. Being a good colleague is part of being a good researcher.

02

Independence without isolation

People need space to develop questions and collaborations that are genuinely their own, together with access to feedback, expertise, mentoring, and practical help.

03

Candour with respect

Psychological safety does not lower standards. It lets unfinished ideas, uncertainty, failed experiments, and disagreement be discussed respectfully; independent routes remain available for concerns.

04

More than papers

Useful research may leave theory, data, code, methods, or documented negative results. We also recognise mentoring, debugging, infrastructure, onboarding, coordination, and care.

These are working commitments. The group reviews what helps, what does not, and what should change. The detailed procedures, current contacts, and onboarding information remain in the internal handbook and Start Here guide.
