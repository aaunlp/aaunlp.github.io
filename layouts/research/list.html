Research

What we study

We investigate how language models work and how they can be used reliably. Our research brings linguistic analysis together with knowledge representation and AI security.

The areas below connect related questions across the group. Individual projects often span more than one.

01

Language & meaning

How do models handle differences between languages?

We use linguistic analysis to understand how models represent meaning and transfer knowledge between languages. This includes formal semantics and typology, as well as evaluation in languages with limited training data.

Our work on language sampling asks which languages a benchmark needs to support a broader claim. Research on information structure examines whether models distinguish what a listener already knows from what a speaker wants to emphasise.

Language sampling and typology →Information structure and accessibility →

Current questions

  • Which linguistic similarities help models transfer knowledge?
  • How should Creole and other lower-resourced language tools be evaluated?
  • Do models recognise how word order and emphasis change meaning?

Faculty: Johannes Bjerva · Heather Lent · Russa Biswas

02

Knowledge & reasoning

How should models represent knowledge and support their claims?

We study how to represent and complete knowledge graphs, combining graph structure with text. We also investigate how models can reason over graph paths and connect generated claims to source documents.

These questions extend to scientific communication: what changes when a finding becomes a summary or a public-facing report, and can readers trace each claim to its evidence?

Knowledge-graph completion →Evidence attribution →

Current questions

  • How can graph structure and text improve knowledge-graph completion?
  • Can generated claims be traced to relevant passages in long documents?
  • How do we measure factuality across languages?

Faculty: Russa Biswas · Dustin Wright · Johannes Bjerva

03

Security & reliability

Where do language models fail or expose private information?

We test privacy leakage and attacks on multilingual language models. Our work includes recovering text from embeddings, distinguishing memorisation from inference, and identifying models that have been tampered with.

We also examine the reliability of evaluation methods. Security experiments need explicit threat models and responsible disclosure; a useful test must establish what it measures and where its conclusions hold.

Heather’s security research →AI:SECURITY →

Current questions

  • Which privacy risks persist across languages and model families?
  • Can linguistic or formal methods reveal hidden model failures?
  • How can security research reduce harm and support responsible disclosure?

Faculty: Johannes Bjerva · Heather Lent · Xikun Jiang · Russa Biswas · Dustin Wright

04

Language technology in use

What changes when language tools are used in practice?

Useful NLP depends on the setting. Creole-language research asks which tools communities need. AI:PAGE connects historical sources so that researchers can explore relationships while retaining the context of the documents.

We also study what AI changes beyond an individual task: whose knowledge appears in model outputs, and the environmental consequences of producing and using those systems.

Diversity-preserving alignment →AI sustainability →

Current questions

  • How should community priorities shape the tasks researchers choose?
  • Can alignment preserve the diversity of ideas expressed by language models?
  • What does sustainable AI require beyond computational efficiency?

Faculty: Heather Lent · Russa Biswas · Dustin Wright · Johannes Bjerva
